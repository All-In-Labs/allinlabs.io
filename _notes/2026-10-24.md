Last week I published ["AI by Default" on LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/iQMzXuNJ3M6EEHZyv/ai-by-default), outlining one possible way the next two years play out that I find especially plausible. It is intended as part of an exercise in thinking through what interventions are most needed in order to ensure development of AGI goes well.

Like everyone else, I've spent much of the last week thinking about cybersecurity, and how AI will continue to alter the cyber landscape. My first dev job involved making websites in PHP, and I have a lot of sympathy for anyone affected by the swathe of vulnerabilities unearthed last week. One key element of the predictions I made in AI by Default was that I expected large-scale, AI-enabled cyberattacks to ramp up significantly in the next 12 months, and, well, I know this wasn't exactly a contrarian take, but it was still vindicating to be proved substantively correct so quickly.
